>
> **First, accessibility.** Traditional feedback often requires a student to physically write on a paper slip or visit a specific administrative office during limited office hours. If you have back-to-back classes or commute, that visit never happens.
>

> **Third, hesitation and confidentiality.** Some students hesitate to raise genuine campus concerns because they fear exposure or judgment if they cannot submit their thoughts in a secure, confidential environment.
>
> **And fourth, administrative overhead.** On the side of our hardworking staff, collecting scattered notes, emails, and verbal complaints makes categorization, prioritization, and follow-ups unnecessarily difficult.
>
> *Without a centralized, organized channel, high-value student suggestions are lost, and recurring campus issues remain invisible.*"

## Slide 4 — How It Works
**Slide Visual:** A clean 5-step lifecycle diagram:  
`1. Submit Feedback` $\rightarrow$ `2. Generate Ref #` $\rightarrow$ `3. Staff Review` $\rightarrow$ `4. Track Progress` $\rightarrow$ `5. Management Insights`.  
**Speaker:** **Member 2**  
**Estimated Time:** 3:15 – 4:15

#### Spoken Script:
> *(Walk through the diagram step by step, using hand gestures to show continuity)*
>
> "Let’s take a look at the end-to-end lifecycle of a suggestion:
>
> **Step 1: Submission.** A student logs into the portal using their institutional credentials. They select their feedback type, choose the department category, write their concern, and can even choose an anonymous toggle for peace of mind.
>
> **Step 2: Instant Reference Generation.** Upon submission, the system generates a standardized reference number—for example, `FB-2026-00124`—giving the student a tangible receipt.
>
> **Step 3: Administrative Triage.** Authorized personnel receive the submission in their dashboard. They can inspect the details, evaluate urgency, update its status, and compose an official response.
>
> **Step 4: Real-time Status Tracking.** The student can view their personal timeline at any moment, seeing exactly when their ticket moved from *Submitted* to *Under Review* or *Resolved*. No guessing games.
>
> **Step 5: Trend & Decision Support.** Management reviews aggregated reports to spot common bottlenecks—for example, if multiple students report air conditioning issues in a specific building, the administration can address the root cause systematically.
>
> Now, what tangible value does this bring to CSPC? Let’s hear from **[Member 3 Name]**."

## Q&A Panel Defense Guide (Anticipated Questions & Answers)

Equip your team with these ready responses during the question-and-answer period:

### Question 1: "How will you prevent students from spamming or submitting prank/offensive feedback?"
> **Recommended Speaker:** Member 5 or Member 2  
> **Strong Answer:**  
> *"That is a very valid point. We address this through three layers of protection:*  
> *1. **Authentication Requirement:** Submissions require students to log in with their institutional credentials (e.g., `@cspc.edu.ph`). Even if they select the anonymous submission option, the system verifies on the backend that the submitter is an active, valid student while keeping their identity masked from regular staff.*  
> *2. **Rate Limiting:** Our backend implements rate limiting, preventing automated spam or multiple repetitive submissions within a short window.*  
> *3. **Terms of Use & Moderation:** Submissions clearly display our campus conduct guidelines, and administrators have the ability to flag or dismiss non-constructive submissions."*

---

### Question 2: "If a submission is anonymous, how can the student be informed of the outcome, or how can staff ask for more details?"
> **Recommended Speaker:** Member 4 or Member 2  
> **Strong Answer:**  
> *"Every submission—anonymous or not—generates a unique tracking reference code (`FB-YYYY-#####`). The student can check the progress and read official administrative responses simply by looking up their ticket or viewing it in their personal dashboard without ever having to disclose their identity publicly."*

---

### Question 3: "Won't this overwhelm administrative staff who already have heavy workloads?"
> **Recommended Speaker:** Member 3 or Member 1  
> **Strong Answer:**  
> *"Actually, the system is designed to reduce staff workload rather than add to it. Right now, staff spend time fielding unorganized verbal complaints, reading disjointed emails, or sorting physical slips. With our platform, feedback arrives pre-categorized by department with built-in search and filtering. Staff can resolve or respond using standardized templates in just a few clicks, turning chaotic feedback into an orderly queue."*

---

### Question 4: "How does the system ensure data privacy and security?"
> **Recommended Speaker:** Member 5  
> **Strong Answer:**  
> *"Data privacy is foundational to our design. We align with the Data Privacy Act of 2012 (RA 10173). Sensitive data is encrypted in transit and at rest, passwords use bcrypt hashing, HTTP security headers are enforced via Helmet, and role-based access control guarantees that only authorized campus personnel can access administrative endpoints."*

---

### Question 5: "What makes your project fit under 'Emerging Technologies' for CCIT 106?"
> **Recommended Speaker:** Member 2 or Member 4  
> **Strong Answer:**  
> *"Our project leverages modern cloud and full-stack web standards (Node.js, Express, RESTful architecture, relational schema optimization) combined with an integrated AI assistant (our Aizen AI module powered by modern LLM technology) that helps students navigate campus services, look up feedback statuses, and escalate urgent concerns dynamically."*

## Solo Presenter Adaptation Notes
If you are presenting by yourself rather than in a group of 5:
1. **Slide 1:** Introduce yourself as the sole lead developer/researcher.
2. **Slide Transitions:** Replace handover lines with smooth connective phrases:
   - *Between Slide 2 & 3:* "Faced with these challenges, I engineered the following solution..."
   - *Between Slide 4 & 5:* "Having seen the architecture, let us examine the strategic value for CSPC..."
   - *Between Slide 5 & 6:* "To prove this concept is ready for implementation, let me demonstrate the live system..."
   - *Between Slide 6 & 7:* "With the prototype operating successfully, the next step is our phased rollout plan..."
3. **Pacing:** Maintain a measured speaking rate of approximately 130–140 words per minute to finish comfortably within the 8-minute mark.
